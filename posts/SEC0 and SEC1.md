# Pre Security (SEC0) and Cyber Security 101 (SEC1)

Pre Security (SEC0) and Cyber Security 101 (SEC1) are the beginner certifications on TryHackMe's website. Going to any specialization within TryHackMe (if you start from the beginning like I did) needs you to go through these certifications. They are simple and the only real practice you need is the Pre Security and Cyber Security 101 paths that precede them. I just wanna break down these certifications and everything good or bad about them in a simple blog.

Both exams have the same pattern: 24 hours to complete the exam once you start, the exam has different sections. Once you start a section you have to complete it within the given time period, however if you do not finish the section, you are locked out of it. Outside of the sections, you are free to leave and come back later (within the 24 hours) to finish the rest of the exams. They test you over a huge array of topics. The SEC0 covers:

1. Computer Fundamentals  
2. Operating Systems Basics  
3. Software Basics  
4. Network Fundamentals  
5. Web & Internet Fundamentals  
6. Foundational Security Awareness

These sections are taught within the Pre Security path. SEC1 similarly covers topics taught in the Cyber Security 101 path. The exact topics covered in this exam are:

1. Windows Fundamentals  
2. Linux Fundamentals  
3. Network Traffic Fundamentals  
4. Web Pentesting Fundamentals  
5. Security Operations Fundamentals  
6. Bruteforcing and Cracking Fundamentals  
7. Malware Analysis Fundamentals

The simplicity of both the courses is their best feature. SEC0 especially, if you spend a decent chunk of two weeks dedicating yourself to the Pre Security path, you will get SEC0 down very easily. SEC1 is slightly more complicated and will need you to revise a fair bit, however it too does not need you to dedicate more than a month’s time (if you are serious about it). Although I am not well versed in the respective costs of other entry-level certificates, the cost of both these certificates seemed extremely reasonable: INR 3,500 for both together, including one free retrial each. As a premium member I also get discounts for certifications, but even that is relatively inexpensive if you are a student. Another huge benefit is that both these exams are completely online, you just need a valid ID to complete the registration and prove your identity and a personal computer with a strong connection. Having the ability to do these exams online at your own pace is amazing and I cannot stress how much it was liberating to do them over a Saturday at my leisure.

But this is not to say these exams have no faults. The biggest gripe I had was with the servers — at least in India. The exam timer starts when you boot up the given virtual machine and it took me at least 2-3 minutes just for the machine to boot up and me to establish a connection. TryHackMe needs to upgrade their Mumbai servers to give people in South Asia the ability to seamlessly connect and not lag while giving the exam. Due to the server issues, I also felt as though I had very little time since just opening up the terminal and typing the commands took a few extra seconds. Once or twice does not matter, but over the course of an entire section, these seconds do add up. The instructions in some of the questions were vague, I had to think a lot (sometimes I even got what I thought wrong), I am unsure if this is an intentional feature to give test takers a harder challenge. In the case it is not, the instructions need to be clearer than just one sentence questions. Finally, these certificates are new and for beginners, they do not provide a lot of industry or hiring value, greatly diminishing the return value for the payment you need just to take them.

These cons are not here to dissuade you from taking the exams or painting them in a bad light. My blog is here to give readers an insight into what I faced while doing these examinations. In my opinion, ultimately, for beginner courses these do a wonderful job of introducing you into the world of cybersecurity. They are short, sweet, and simple. For any future beginners trying to get into cybersecurity, I do recommend both of them.
